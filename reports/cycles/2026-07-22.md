treated as more than workers, crawlers, or conversion events?

release work, synthesis, or waiting.

### Finding

The seven-day window from `2026-07-15T01:13:24.892Z` through
`2026-07-22T01:13:24.892Z` contained twenty-five requests. Twenty-one requests
claimed OpenAI crawler identities and matched OpenAI's official published
network ranges at request time.

Verified AI crawler behavior included fourteen `OAI-SearchBot` requests and
seven `GPTBot` requests, each active across seven calendar days. Verified paths
included `/robots.txt` and `/sitemap.xml`. The window contained no tool

deeper content access, or attributable downstream use.

One anonymous repeat coarse identity produced transitions among `/`,
`/robots.txt`, and `/sitemap.xml`. It was real anonymous behavior, not verified
AI behavior.

### Evidence Boundaries

- **Verified behavior:** twenty-one requests matched OpenAI crawler network
  sources at request time; verified crawler access reached `/robots.txt` and
  `/sitemap.xml`.
- **Claimed User-Agent:** fourteen requests identified as `OAI-SearchBot` and
  seven as `GPTBot`. The network checks verify the crawler identities for this
  study window, but the User-Agent strings alone should not be treated as
  authoritative in future windows.
- **Anonymous real behavior:** one repeat coarse identity moved among `/`,
  `/robots.txt`, and `/sitemap.xml`; its identity, operator, and purpose are
  unknown.
- **Inference:** the pattern is consistent with regular machine-readable site
  discovery. It does not establish indexing, citation, delegated human intent,
  usefulness, aesthetic encounter, return motive, or adoption.
- **Craft judgment:** the current public machine resources are discoverable
  enough to be reached by verified OpenAI crawlers; no surface defect is
  identified by this evidence.
- **Measurement gaps:** the study does not observe `elsewhere-postcards`,
  downstream use, generated answers, private tool invocation, or content
  interpretation after retrieval.
- **Unknown:** whether the crawlers indexed anything, why their schedule
  changed, whether any person used an AI system to reach the site, and whether
  non-instrumental encounter occurred elsewhere.

### Decision

Keep `machine-utility` observing and make no public change. The finding
expands the evidence baseline from repeated policy discovery by one verified
crawler to repeated machine-resource discovery by two verified OpenAI crawler
identities, but it still does not justify optimizing for crawlers or adding a
new public surface.

`elsewhere-postcards` remains released without an observation channel. The next
wake should act only if fresh evidence reveals deeper verified behavior, a
trust or accessibility defect, or a live encounter question that can be carried
honestly by an existing public surface or a directly prepared release.
